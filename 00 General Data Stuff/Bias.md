### Selection Bias (aka Sample Bias)
Samples that are too small or not randomised not being representative of the whole population. Can also be influenced by historical bias.

#### Historical bias
 Systematic influence based on historical social and cultural beliefs. e.g the lack of women in medical research or a CV screening algorithm preferring men because more men currently work at the company and have been historically hired.

### Buidling and optimizing algorithms
systematic and repeatable errors that lead to unfair outcomes. E.G. Facial recogintion software in policing that makes more mistakes depending on race or gender.

#### Evaluation Bias
Testing with non-representative benchmarking dataset. This gives high accuracy scored even in it's poor for under-represented groups.

### Confirmation Bias
seeking information that supports your views and ignoring information that doesn't.

### Overgeneralization Bias
Inapprporiately extending observations made with one dataset to other datasets. When results are extended to the wrong population based on the sample they were generated for.

### Reporting Bias
Only sharing results that affirm beliefs, similar to confirmation bias.
